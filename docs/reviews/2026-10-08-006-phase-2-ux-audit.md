# Phase 2 product and UX audit

## 1. Executive summary

### Closure assessment

The current experience is not coherent enough to close Phase 2 as a product
experience. The implementation contains the required financial facts and many
good local interactions, but a new person is still expected to discover the
setup sequence, reconcile conflicting answers, and understand the relationship
between nine settings pages.

The recommendation is: **Not ready to close; requires a focused UX increment.**
This does not reopen Phase 2 calculations or ask for a redesign. The smallest
useful increment is to make Plan the honest coordinator of the existing facts:
show affordability before payoff optimism, identify the next missing or
corrective action, and link directly to the existing editor that resolves it.

### Biggest usability risk

Plan can give a reassuring answer that conflicts with its own cash forecast.
With the inspected household, the summary says:

> You're on track to be debt-free paying only your minimums.

The same page says cash runs short on November 1, 2026 and reaches
`-$41,194.11` by April 1, 2028. Living separately says the shared plan is short
`$2,516.91` a month. The payoff sentence is mathematically about debt
amortization, but a beginner will read "on track" as an affordability judgment.
The cash warning appears after the debt composition, balance, minimum, and
payoff-order sections, especially far below the first screen on a phone.

This is a P0 UX and trust defect. The audit did not establish that either
calculation is wrong. It established that the product presents incompatible
meanings as one plan.

### Biggest scope risk

The product is exposing its data model as a suite of destinations. Income,
Bills, Debts, Living, Savings, Targets, Categories, Connections, and Household
all live in one settings list. Several are valid editors, but Cardui does not
yet coordinate them into a first-plan journey. A future assistant would have to
explain where facts live and in which order to enter them, rather than helping
with a financial decision.

The screen-level implementation is broader than the journey-level product. The
risk is continuing to add scenario pages or assistant tools before the existing
facts produce one understandable answer.

### What is working well

- The light shell, typography, cards, panels, themed pickers, and responsive
  component tree are calm and largely consistent.
- Accounts and Activity support direct manual use as well as connected data.
- Income preserves dated paychecks and keeps gross, low, strong, and expected
  raises separate from typical net pay.
- Debt setup allows unknown terms instead of coercing blanks to zero.
- Followed debts preserve field ownership: balance and credit limit can be
  synced while APR, minimum, and due date remain person-maintained.
- Living now has one canonical flexible-spending amount instead of a duplicate
  allowance model.
- Savings clearly states that saving a goal does not move money.
- Loading fallbacks, page-level API banners, local empty states, destructive
  confirmations, and stale followed-debt states already provide useful
  foundations.

## 2. First-use journey

### 1. Sign in and arrive

The sign-in route renders the Clerk form on a blank light page. The signed-in
shell identifies the product as **Tortoise — Personal ledger**, while the
roadmap defines Cardui as a financial recovery product. There is no first-use
sentence that explains the recovery outcome.

After authentication there is no explicit redirect. The root route is Home,
and the primary navigation is intentionally Home, Accounts, Activity, Plan.
Plan's fourth position is correct and is not a finding.

A new empty Home offers only "Link a bank to start tracking your net worth."
Manual accounts are supported from Accounts, but that option is not offered in
Home's first empty state. The first impression is therefore a connected
personal-finance tracker, not a manual-or-connected recovery planner.

Likely hesitation: "Is this an account tracker, or is it going to help me make a
plan?"

### 2. Understand the navigation

The four primary destinations are understandable individually. The account
menu is not. On a phone, tapping the email opens nine ungrouped destinations.
Income, Bills, Debts, Living, and Savings are core planning inputs, but their
placement under an account control makes them resemble preferences.

Likely hesitation: "Where do I set up the plan, and why are debts and income in
my account menu?"

The shell rule should remain: do not add a fifth tab. Plan should expose the
setup sequence contextually so the account menu can remain a secondary editor
map.

### 3. Add or review accounts and transactions

Accounts has a clear Add account action and then offers Enter manually or
Connect with Plaid. Activity has Import CSV and Add transaction. Account rows
and transaction rows are easy to scan, and a row preserves context by opening
a detail panel.

Friction:

- Home's empty state hides the manual route that Accounts supports.
- Account rows display raw subtype values such as `creditcard`, `paypal`, and
  `_401k`. These expose provider or storage vocabulary instead of user labels.
- Account and Home totals do not show their data freshness. Freshness is
  available on Connections and on followed debts, but not beside the totals
  Plan uses as starting cash.

### 4. Add income

The Income list makes one-payment amounts and upcoming dates visible. The form
puts gross, low, strong, and raises behind disclosure, which is appropriate.

Friction:

- Steady, low, strong, reliability, contributor assignment, and expected
  raises are not connected to one visible sentence explaining what Plan uses.
- A person is created on Household, attached to pay on Income, and assigned a
  shared portion on Living. The relationship is correct in the data but not
  presented as one journey.
- There is no household income summary or direct link from Income to the
  contribution decision.

Likely hesitation: "I entered my paycheck. Is all of it now available to the
plan?"

### 5. Add debts

Debts is the strongest inventory page. It shows balance, balance source,
freshness, limit, utilization, APR, minimum, due date, and monthly interest.
Unknown terms can remain unknown. Followed fields and person-maintained fields
are technically separate.

Confidence breaks in the inspected data:

- Debts reports `$816.95` of minimums and six rates at 20% or more.
- Plan pays `$694.58` a month and includes four positive-balance debts.
- A zero-balance followed card still shows a `$122.37` minimum.
- Zero-balance cards are included in the high-rate count.

The arithmetic difference is explainable from the rows, but the product does
not explain it. A beginner cannot tell whether the zero-balance card still has
a payment due, whether the debt is closed, or which total is authoritative.

The most prominent row actions are Stop following, Edit, and Remove. They are
maintenance actions, not debt-recovery actions. Active and zero-balance debts
share one undifferentiated list.

### 6. Understand Plan

Plan currently answers two different questions:

1. When do the entered debt balances amortize if the entered debt payments
   continue?
2. Does the household have enough dated cash to make those payments while
   covering bills, living spending, and savings?

The first answer is presented as the headline. The second can directly
contradict it but appears after four analytical sections. There is no
consolidated "what to do next" section.

The path controls and charts are useful analysis, but they precede the decision
the user needs: whether the next month works and what input or action matters
now. "Rollover" is also planner vocabulary; "Keep paying the same total" would
better predict the result.

### 7. Configure living spending and savings

Living clearly distinguishes scheduled Bills from one flexible monthly amount.
It also shows monthly affordability. That is useful.

The contribution rule is difficult to predict. In the inspected household, an
entered `$300` benchmark is described as `$300.04` available to the plan
because the stored benchmark becomes a share that is reapplied to paycheck
schedules. The four-cent difference is explainable by averaging, but it makes a
saved user amount appear not to be preserved.

Savings separates Cash to keep, Emergency, and named dated goals. However:

- The page's primary action is Save for something, which gives an optional
  named goal more prominence than establishing a safe foundation.
- It calculates monthly goal amounts without saying whether the combined goals
  are affordable.
- Cash to keep and Emergency are additive in Plan, but the page does not say
  whether the floor is in addition to emergency savings. A user can reasonably
  assume one contains the other.
- In the inspected household, Savings proposes `$522.95` a month for Emergency
  and `$1,111.11` a month for a named goal while Living already reports a
  `$2,516.91` monthly shortfall.

### 8. Understand household contributions

The current calculation is coherent once learned: Household defines people,
Income assigns pay, and Living limits the share of that pay that enters the
shared plan. It is not discoverable without reading three pages.

"Living" does not predict that it contains household contribution rules.
"Financial profile" does not match the navigation label "Household." The
stored facts have one owner each, but the mental model is fragmented.

### 9. Recover from incomplete, stale, or inconsistent data

Strong states:

- Page loaders render skeletons.
- Server loaders keep a page renderable and show a page API error banner.
- Empty Income, Bills, Debts, Plan, Targets, Connections, and savings slots
  provide a local action.
- Followed debts retain stale values and explain refresh, reconnect, override,
  and stop-following actions.

Weak states:

- The app has no page-level setup status that distinguishes "not entered,"
  "known zero," "stale," and "complete enough for this answer."
- Plan does not display the freshness or source of starting cash.
- A successful debt payoff projection is not downgraded when the cash forecast
  is unaffordable.
- Home and Accounts do not state when connected totals were last refreshed.
- Partial server failures use empty fallback collections under a banner. The
  banner is helpful, but the screen below can still resemble a real empty
  household.

### 10. Return later

Home answers "what happened" through net worth, this month, and recent
activity. Plan answers "what the current saved facts project." Neither answers
"what changed since my last visit" or "what action matters next."

Actual-versus-plan review is correctly scheduled later in Phase 5. It should
not be pulled wholesale into this fix. The Phase 2 foundation still needs a
small current-state next action and visible fact freshness so a returning user
can trust today's answer.

## 3. Information architecture audit

### Shell and primary navigation

Keep four primary destinations. Do not add a fifth tab.

Approved order:

1. Home
2. Accounts
3. Activity
4. Plan

Current code renders that order correctly on desktop and phone. The former
Plan-first rule, design text, and source comments were documentation drift, not
an implementation defect.

### Product framing

"Personal ledger" and the metadata description "Personal finance tracking" do
not match the roadmap mission. The wording makes the broad tracking surfaces
feel like the product and Plan feel optional.

Recommended direction: one short signed-in descriptor that names planning or
financial recovery. This is a product-language decision, not a request for a
marketing site.

### Settings map

Keep the existing account-menu location to remain consistent with the approved
shell, but reduce the amount of knowledge required to use it:

- Plan should link to each fact that needs review.
- Group the menu visually into plan inputs and account/data settings, without
  adding routes.
- Keep Income, Bills, and Debts as separate pages; each has a distinct list,
  form, and maintenance lifecycle.
- Keep Connections, Categories, and Household as secondary settings.
- Keep Savings separate, but subordinate named goals until the foundation is
  set.
- Rename Living to a term that predicts both available household pay and
  flexible spending. "Plan budget" is the clearest candidate found in this
  audit.
- Rename Targets to "Spending targets" if the label can fit. It is an activity
  tracking tool, not another Plan input.

### Page boundaries

No large page merge is justified. Combining Income, Bills, Debts, Savings, and
Targets would create a long setup form and make maintenance harder.

The missing boundary is a coordinator, not another editor. Plan should contain
a compact setup/readiness section and contextual links. That section should
summarize existing sources of truth; it should not copy their fields or create
another saved model.

Household contributor identity should remain on Household. The effect of a
contributor's pay share should be explained and linked from Income and Plan.

### Context preservation

Record edits generally preserve context through right-hand panels and
full-screen phone sheets. This is working.

Exceptions:

- Connections is a top-level settings destination but renders a back control.
  The governance rule reserves back for nested flows.
- Finish-your-plan rows link only to the Debts page rather than opening the
  relevant debt. That was explicitly out of scope in the approved design, but
  it adds search work and should be reconsidered for a focused correction.
- Plan has no direct links to Income or Savings when those values exist but
  need review.

## 4. Screen-by-screen audit

### Sign in

- **User goal:** Access the product and understand what they are entering.
- **Current mental model:** Generic Clerk authentication.
- **Primary action:** Sign in or sign up.
- **Main confusion:** No product purpose accompanies the form.
- **Severity:** P2 UX.
- **Evidence:** `frontend/app/sign-in/[[...sign-in]]/page.tsx` renders only
  `<SignIn />` in a centered container. Signed-out visual QA was not performed.
- **Recommendation:** Add one concise product-purpose sentence around the
  existing auth component; do not build onboarding here.

### Home

- **User goal:** Understand where the household stands and what changed.
- **Current mental model:** Personal finance dashboard.
- **Primary action:** None for a populated household; Link a bank when empty.
- **Main confusion:** It does not point toward a first plan, and the empty state
  hides manual account entry.
- **Severity:** P1 UX.
- **Evidence:** `DashboardNetWorthCard.tsx` offers only `PlaidLinkButton` when
  no accounts exist. The inspected populated route showed net worth, this
  month, and recent activity with no plan-readiness or next-action cue.
- **Recommendation:** Offer Add account with manual-or-connected choices in the
  empty state. Keep Home historical; add only a quiet Plan next-action link if
  a plan needs attention.

### Accounts

- **User goal:** See balances and add or maintain accounts.
- **Current mental model:** One inventory of cash, investments, cards, and
  loans.
- **Primary action:** Add account.
- **Main confusion:** Raw subtypes and absent freshness reduce clarity.
- **Severity:** P2 UX.
- **Evidence:** `AccountListRow.tsx` prints `account.subtype ??
  account.type`; the inspected page showed `creditcard`, `paypal`, and `_401k`.
- **Recommendation:** Map stored types to user labels and show aggregate
  freshness when connected data contributes to totals.

### Activity

- **User goal:** Find, add, import, and correct transactions.
- **Current mental model:** A ledger with filters and detail panels.
- **Primary action:** Add transaction; Import CSV is secondary.
- **Main confusion:** The empty panel does not repeat the action that fills it,
  and filter refreshes keep old rows without clearly saying they are updating.
- **Severity:** P2 technical/accessibility; the core page model is clear.
- **Evidence:** The inspected populated route had search, account/category
  filters, date groups, status badges, and row detail behavior.
  `TransactionsClient.tsx` also renders a second `<main>` inside the shell's
  `<main>`, and only the filter control exposes `aria-busy` during a refresh.
- **Recommendation:** Leave the page boundary and action hierarchy alone.
  Remove the nested landmark, add a visible updating state, and repeat Add
  transaction in the truly empty state.

### Income

- **User goal:** Record when reliable take-home pay arrives.
- **Current mental model:** A list of pay sources and scenarios.
- **Primary action:** Add income.
- **Main confusion:** The effect of contributor assignment, reliability, and
  low/strong values on Plan is not summarized.
- **Severity:** P2 UX.
- **Evidence:** The inspected rows show cadence, contributor, reliability,
  typical payment, optional scenarios, dates, and monthly average, but no
  "used by Plan" summary.
- **Recommendation:** Add a short Plan-use sentence and a link to the
  contribution rule. Keep optional details disclosed.

### Bills

- **User goal:** Record non-debt payments that recur.
- **Current mental model:** A scheduled-payment list with optional suggestions.
- **Primary action:** Add bill.
- **Main confusion:** The page does not summarize the monthly burden or warn
  against entering debt payments here.
- **Severity:** P2 UX.
- **Evidence:** The populated route showed one row and no summary. The Living
  design, not the page, carries the rule that debt payments belong on Debts.
- **Recommendation:** Add a small normalized monthly total and one sentence
  that debt payments belong on Debts. Keep suggestion confirmation unchanged.

### Debts

- **User goal:** Know what is owed, which terms are known, and what requires
  attention.
- **Current mental model:** Detailed inventory and sync-maintenance console.
- **Primary action:** Add debt.
- **Main confusion:** Zero-balance debts affect summary signals and minimums;
  maintenance actions dominate; "Revolving," "APR," and "Synced" require
  interpretation.
- **Severity:** P1 UX/trust.
- **Evidence:** Runtime totals and rows described in the first-use journey;
  `debtOptions.ts` labels the two types only as Revolving and Installment;
  `DebtList.tsx` places Stop following, Edit, and Remove on every card.
- **Recommendation:** Separate active from zero-balance debts, explain summary
  exclusions, expand jargon, state exactly which fields are synced, and move
  rare maintenance into the existing action-menu pattern.

### Living

- **User goal:** Decide how much household pay Plan may use and how much
  flexible spending to preserve.
- **Current mental model:** A household-plan budget once the page is read.
- **Primary action:** Multiple per-contributor Save buttons; Edit monthly
  spending.
- **Main confusion:** "Living" does not predict contribution rules, and a saved
  benchmark can display a few cents differently after conversion to a share.
- **Severity:** P1 terminology; P2 predictability.
- **Evidence:** Inspected `$300` input was explained as `$300.04` shared; the
  page separately showed a `$2,516.91` monthly shortfall.
- **Recommendation:** Rename the page, preserve or explicitly explain the exact
  benchmark-to-share rounding, and let its shortfall control Plan's headline
  state.

### Savings

- **User goal:** Protect cash and prepare for dated costs.
- **Current mental model:** Three kinds of savings that all affect Plan.
- **Primary action:** Save for something.
- **Main confusion:** Optional goals outrank the cash foundation; affordability
  and additive reserve behavior are not explained.
- **Severity:** P1 UX.
- **Evidence:** Runtime monthly goal amounts coexist with the Living shortfall;
  `SavingsPageClient.tsx` always places Save for something in the title row.
- **Recommendation:** When Cash to keep or Emergency is unset, make that setup
  the visible next action. Explain that Cash to keep is added to emergency and
  named reserves if that remains the rule. Show an affordability warning from
  the existing monthly gap rather than inventing another calculation.

### Spending targets

- **User goal:** Compare categorized posted spending with monthly intentions.
- **Current mental model:** A second budget-like system.
- **Primary action:** Choose a month, then choose a category.
- **Main confusion:** It resembles the spending input that Plan should use but
  explicitly does not affect Plan.
- **Severity:** P2 UX/scope.
- **Evidence:** The inspected page showed 16 categories with no target and
  `$15,800.22` of untargeted spending while its description says targets do not
  add another amount to Plan.
- **Recommendation:** Rename to Spending targets, keep it out of first-plan
  setup, and continue labeling it as tracking only. When there are no spending
  categories, link directly to Categories. Do not merge targets into forecast
  inputs.

### Plan

- **User goal:** Know whether the plan works and what to do next.
- **Current mental model:** Debt payoff calculator plus a separate cash
  forecast.
- **Primary action:** Try extra each month or switch payoff paths.
- **Main confusion:** The headline can contradict affordability; analysis
  precedes action; no next payment or next fact is identified.
- **Severity:** P0 UX/trust.
- **Evidence:** Runtime contradiction and component order described above.
  `planSummary()` branches on debt payoff only. `PlanPageClient.tsx` renders
  Cash outlook after the debt charts and payoff order.
- **Recommendation:** Make plan status a combined affordability and payoff
  state. Put cash safety and one next action before charts. Label the date as an
  estimate and distinguish scheduled facts, assumptions, and tried scenarios.

### Connections

- **User goal:** Connect, refresh, repair, or remove linked banks.
- **Current mental model:** Technical data-source maintenance.
- **Primary action:** Connect account; Sync all is secondary.
- **Main confusion:** A top-level page has a back control, and per-bank actions
  create a dense maintenance surface.
- **Severity:** P2 technical/governance.
- **Evidence:** `InstitutionsPageClient.tsx` passes `showBack` even though
  Connections is in the account-menu map.
- **Recommendation:** Remove the top-level back affordance in a future approved
  fix. Standardize the visible terms Connections, banks, and institutions so
  one concept has one user-facing name. Keep per-bank freshness and repair
  behavior.

### Household

- **User goal:** Set planning currency, time zone, and household people.
- **Current mental model:** "Financial profile," despite a Household nav label.
- **Primary action:** Save profile; contributor rows have their own saves.
- **Main confusion:** The page does not explain that contributors connect
  Income to Living contribution shares.
- **Severity:** P2 UX.
- **Evidence:** Runtime heading and `HouseholdPageClient.tsx`.
- **Recommendation:** Match the title to Household, explain downstream use, and
  use the shared Switch primitive for the two-state Shown setting.

## 5. End-to-end flow audit

### Initial setup

There is no single first-use sequence. Local empty states are individually
helpful but can send the person into a dead-end order. Home starts with bank
linking, while a meaningful plan also needs debts, income, bills, flexible
spending, and a cash decision.

Recommended sequence:

1. Add accounts manually or connect them.
2. Add debts and known terms.
3. Add take-home income and dates.
4. Add recurring non-debt bills.
5. Set plan-available household pay and flexible monthly spending.
6. Choose cash to keep; add an emergency target only when useful.
7. Review one honest Plan status and next action.

The sequence should be represented on Plan, not as a new onboarding route.

### Accounts

Account creation and transaction entry are complete enough for Phase 2.
Manual and connected paths coexist correctly. The main gaps are first-use
discoverability, friendly account-type labels, and freshness.

### Income

The facts are rich enough, but their use is distributed. Typical pay drives
the normal forecast, low pay drives the disclosed alternative, and Living may
reduce the shared share. These rules should be visible together before a user
is asked to reason about raises or strong pay.

### Debts

The inventory is technically capable but does not yet produce one actionable
debt answer. Terms, utilization, and sync provenance are visible; active debt
status, summary inclusion, and next payment are not.

### Plan

Plan is the main closure blocker. It must not claim the household is on track
when the cash model says the entered payments are not affordable. A debt-free
date can remain as a conditional estimate, subordinate to the condition that
cash flow works.

### Savings

Cash to keep and emergency savings belong in the plan foundation. Named goals
are useful but should remain progressive disclosure until the foundation is
set or the plan can show the tradeoff. Goal creation must remain user-directed;
Cardui should not recommend a universal emergency target.

### Household contributions

Unequal contributions are appropriately supported, but the current three-page
path is too implicit. The product should show the relationship without adding
another source of truth.

### Returning users

Home provides useful historical orientation. Plan lacks freshness and a
current next action. Full actual-versus-plan history should stay in Phase 5,
but current fact freshness and current blocking action are needed now.

### Missing or stale data

Missing debt terms and stale followed debts are handled best. Starting cash,
account totals, plan inputs, and partially failed server loads are less clear.
The product needs a consistent vocabulary:

- **Fact:** saved or connected amount, with source and date where relevant.
- **Unknown:** not supplied; never treated as confirmed zero.
- **Estimate:** calculated from stated assumptions.
- **Scenario:** temporary user-selected alternative.
- **Recommendation:** an action Cardui proposes, with reason and tradeoff.

## 6. AI-readiness audit

### Clear concepts and vocabulary

**Partially ready.** The domain records are well separated, but user vocabulary
is not. Living, Targets, Cash to keep, Emergency, Rollover, Revolving, and
Financial profile require interpretation. An assistant would have to translate
the navigation before discussing the plan.

### Stable facts and sources of truth

**Mostly ready in code, not ready in presentation.** Accounts, income, bills,
debts, savings goals, and contribution shares each have a canonical stored
home. The main risk is perceived duplication:

- Account liability versus debt record.
- Cash to keep versus emergency reserve.
- Living spending versus spending targets.
- Debt summary minimums versus Plan payment amount.

The UI must explain these distinctions so an assistant does not become the
only source of that explanation.

### Understandable assumptions

**Not ready.** Plan has an assumptions disclosure, but the decisive
affordability condition is not reflected in its headline. Exact distant dates
are visually stronger than the assumptions that make them possible.

### Obvious user decisions

**Not ready.** Users can edit facts, but Plan does not identify the one decision
that matters next. Savings asks for target amounts and dates before helping the
person judge feasibility.

### Reviewable proposed changes

**Foundation is promising.** Temporary extra payment is explicitly unsaved,
and forms require user submission. There is no saved-plan proposal model yet,
which correctly remains later roadmap work.

### Facts, estimates, and recommendations

**Not ready.** The product distinguishes some synced and manual debt fields,
but the plan headline does not distinguish a conditional payoff estimate from
an affordability conclusion. Recommendations do not yet exist as a separate
object or visual layer.

### Product-model simplicity

**Too complex at the journey level.** The persisted model is reasonable. The
navigation requires the user to compose it manually. AI integration should
wait until Plan can explain the same model without AI.

## 7. Findings ranked by priority

### P0 — Plan reassurance contradicts cash affordability

- **Priority:** P0.
- **Type:** UX/trust defect.
- **User impact:** A person can believe the plan is viable while the app
  predicts an immediate and growing cash deficit.
- **Evidence:** Runtime Plan summary, Cash outlook, and Living shortfall;
  `planCopy.ts` calculates the headline from payoff-path state only.
- **Recommended direction:** Combined plan status. If cash goes short, lead
  with "This payoff path is not affordable yet," retain the payoff date as a
  conditional estimate, and identify the first corrective input or decision.
- **Fix before Phase 2 closure:** Yes.

### P1 — No first-plan journey or current next action

- **Priority:** P1.
- **Type:** UX and scope defect.
- **User impact:** A beginner must discover setup order across nine settings
  and translate charts into an action.
- **Evidence:** No onboarding/setup/next-step implementation was found; Plan
  links missing debts, income, and living only in isolated states.
- **Recommended direction:** A compact readiness/next-action section on Plan
  backed by existing facts and links.
- **Fix before Phase 2 closure:** Yes.

### P1 — The shell frames the product as a ledger

- **Priority:** P1.
- **Type:** Product-positioning defect.
- **User impact:** The product appears to be a tracker; the recovery outcome
  looks secondary.
- **Evidence:** "Personal ledger" brand line and metadata "Personal finance
  tracking." The approved Home, Accounts, Activity, Plan order is not part of
  this finding.
- **Recommended direction:** Keep the current navigation order and approve one
  recovery-oriented descriptor.
- **Fix before Phase 2 closure:** Yes.

### P1 — Debt and Plan payment totals are not reconciled

- **Priority:** P1.
- **Type:** UX/trust defect; calculation correctness not disproven.
- **User impact:** The user cannot know which payment amount is due or why a
  zero-balance debt still contributes a minimum.
- **Evidence:** `$816.95` on Debts versus `$694.58` on Plan; zero-balance card
  with `$122.37` minimum; high-rate count includes zero-balance debts.
- **Recommended direction:** Separate active and zero-balance debts, explain
  inclusion rules beside both totals, and prompt review rather than silently
  deleting saved terms.
- **Fix before Phase 2 closure:** Yes.

### P1 — Plan does not show source freshness

- **Priority:** P1.
- **Type:** UX/trust defect.
- **User impact:** An exact forecast may use connected cash or debt balances
  whose age is visible elsewhere but not on Plan.
- **Evidence:** `cashOutlook.asOf` exists in the API shape but is not rendered;
  account sync dates live on Connections; followed-debt dates live on Debts.
- **Recommended direction:** Show "Based on facts as of …" and name stale or
  manually maintained inputs that materially affect the answer.
- **Fix before Phase 2 closure:** Yes, at summary level; full freshness
  operations remain later.

### P1 — Core planning concepts are fragmented

- **Priority:** P1.
- **Type:** Information-architecture defect.
- **User impact:** Users must understand Household → Income → Living and
  Living → Savings → Targets relationships before they can interpret Plan.
- **Evidence:** Runtime account menu and page copy.
- **Recommended direction:** Contextual coordination on Plan; rename Living;
  group menu items; do not duplicate stored fields.
- **Fix before Phase 2 closure:** Yes, with copy and links rather than a page
  merge.

### P1 — Savings goals ignore visible affordability

- **Priority:** P1.
- **Type:** UX defect.
- **User impact:** The page can ask for substantial monthly saving while the
  current plan already has a shortfall.
- **Evidence:** Runtime Emergency and named-goal monthly amounts versus Living
  shortfall.
- **Recommended direction:** Surface the existing affordability status and
  prioritize Cash to keep/Emergency setup before optional named goals.
- **Fix before Phase 2 closure:** Yes.

### P2 — Cash to keep and Emergency can look like duplicate reserves

- **Priority:** P2.
- **Type:** Product-language decision.
- **User impact:** A user may not know whether the two amounts are additive or
  overlapping.

## 8. Simplification proposal

### Primary user journey

1. Arrive at Home and see one sentence explaining Cardui's purpose and a clear
   route to the first planning action.
2. Open Plan when ready to build or review the recovery path.
3. Plan shows the smallest missing or unsafe item, with one action.
4. The action opens or links to the existing Income, Bills, Debts, Plan budget,
   or Savings editor.
5. Return to Plan and see a combined affordability/payoff answer.
6. Use Accounts and Activity to maintain facts over time.
7. Return later to Home for history and Plan for today's planning action.

### Minimum destinations

Primary destinations remain:

- Home
- Accounts
- Activity
- Plan

Secondary editors remain:

- Income
- Bills
- Debts
- Plan budget (renamed Living)
- Savings
- Spending targets (renamed Targets)
- Categories
- Connections
- Household

The simplification is not fewer persisted features. It is fewer destinations a
new user must discover unaided.

### Screens that remain

- Keep Accounts and Activity unchanged in purpose.
- Keep Income, Bills, and Debts as focused editors.
- Keep Savings as protected cash and dated goals.
- Keep Targets as activity tracking only.
- Keep Connections, Categories, and Household as secondary settings.

### Concepts to merge or move

- Merge the *status*, not the forms: Plan should summarize income, bills, debt
  payments, flexible spending, protected cash, and resulting cash.
- Move setup sequencing and next action onto Plan.
- Move the explanation of contribution effects next to Income and Plan through
  links and summary copy.
- Do not add another budget model or copy target values into Living.

### Progressive disclosure

- Keep low/strong income, gross pay, raises, promotional debt terms, and
  calculation rules disclosed.
- Hide named savings-goal creation behind the foundation when Cash to keep or
  Emergency is unset.
- Put debt link maintenance and removal in a secondary row menu.
- Keep long-range charts after the next action and near-term cash result.

### Features to defer

- Saved plan versions, action history, and actual-versus-plan review.
- AI conversation.
- Additional scenarios beyond the approved roadmap order.
- Full stale-data operations across every aggregate.
- New scores, dashboards, or educational content systems.

### Obvious next action

- **New user:** "Add the next fact your first plan needs," beginning with a
  manual-or-connected account or a debt, depending on what is missing.
- **Returning user:** "Review the first stale, inconsistent, or unaffordable
  assumption," otherwise show the next debt payment and cash remaining.
